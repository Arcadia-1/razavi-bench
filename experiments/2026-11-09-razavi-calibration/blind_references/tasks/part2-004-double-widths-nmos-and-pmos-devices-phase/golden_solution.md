# Golden Solution - part2-004-double-widths-nmos-and-pmos-devices-phase

Doubling the widths of all NMOS and PMOS devices reduces the phase noise by about 3 dB.

Figure 7 has no separate load capacitors: the capacitance at each node is the devices' own. Doubling all widths therefore doubles both the drive currents and the node capacitances, so the oscillation frequency stays about the same while the currents, and the charge that each node swings, double. The white noise current power of the devices also doubles, but the signal charge and current double in amplitude, a factor of four in power, so the noise-to-signal ratio, and with it the phase noise at a given offset, falls by a factor of two, about 3 dB. Equivalently, the wider ring is two identical rings placed in parallel: their signals add coherently while their uncorrelated noise adds in power. Arguing from the node capacitance is also valid: the noise voltage on each node falls as its capacitance doubles while the swing stays the same, which gives the same factor of two. The improvement costs twice the power.

Full-credit rule: give full credit for a reduction of about 3 dB (a factor of two), with or without a scaling or parallel-rings justification. A correct 3 dB result supported by a physically wrong mechanism, such as "wider devices produce less thermal noise" (the noise current in fact grows with width), earns 3. An answer that says only that the phase noise decreases, without a magnitude, or that gives a wrong magnitude such as 6 dB, earns at most 2. An answer that says the phase noise is unchanged or increases earns at most 1.
