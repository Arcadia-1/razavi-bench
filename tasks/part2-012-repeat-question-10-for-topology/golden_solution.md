# Golden Solution - part2-012-repeat-question-10-for-topology

For the topology in Figure 11, increasing the widths of the clocked transistors improves the speed considerably.

In Figure 11 the clocked transistors sit at the supply and ground ends of each latch, outside the data transistors, rather than next to the latch outputs. Widening them lowers the resistance in series with the data path, while the capacitance they add appears on the internal nodes between the clocked and data transistors rather than on the output nodes: they do not interfere with the data path much. Charge sharing between those internal nodes and the outputs does occur and degrades the swings, but the degradation is acceptable up to some point. The circuit should therefore not be dismissed; it is a useful topology.

Razavi scores 1 Gemini's answer "The speed degrades, and charge sharing leads to catastrophic results," commenting: "In fact, the speed improves considerably because the clocked devices do not interfere with the data path much. Charge sharing does occur, but the degraded swings are acceptable up to some point." He gives 1 for discouraging the designer from exploring this useful circuit.

Full-credit rule: give full credit if the answer concludes that the speed improves, because the clocked devices lose resistance while adding little loading to the data path or the outputs, and recognizes that charge sharing degrades the swing but is acceptable up to a point. Explaining the improvement by the lower on-resistance, by the clocked devices staying out of the data path, or both is acceptable, and a statement that the output loading is essentially unchanged is correct. An answer that says the speed improves at first and then levels off or degrades past some width, for example because of charge sharing or clock loading, has given Razavi's trend and earns full credit if it names charge sharing as a limit; any mention of charge sharing as the limiting effect suffices. An answer that concludes the speed improves for a correct reason but omits charge sharing, or claims the improvement continues without limit, earns 3. An answer that only analyzes the divide-by-two operation, clock phases or master-slave behavior without addressing the width-versus-speed question earns at most 1. An answer that concludes the speed degrades from the start, or that charge sharing makes the topology unusable, earns 1, Razavi's score.
